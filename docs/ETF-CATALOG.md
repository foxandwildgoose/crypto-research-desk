# Crypto ETF lifecycle catalog

The market scope is currently **US ETF/ETP products and filings**. The catalog is not a claim of global completeness. Existing ByKaranteli tables are secondary flow observations; a reported number is not SEC approval or proof of spot exposure.

`desk.etf.catalog.records` contains product identities and lifecycle evidence. `desk.etf.products`, `sessions`, and `heat` retain their existing measurement contract. This separation is necessary: a proposed fund has no flow row, and a flow row has no authoritative filing status. No database or migration is needed for this snapshot/server-cache architecture.

## Source workflow

1. Discover registration/prospectus records via EDGAR full/daily indexes or current filings, without a coin whitelist. Inspect individual fund series; a trust may contain multiple products. EFTS may help discovery, but a search result is not a current-status feed.
2. Read original SEC filings/orders, exchange notices, and issuer product pages. Identify underlying assets and exposure from the document, not the ticker. Follow amendments, withdrawal requests, and launch notices before assigning a status **as of a specific date**. S-1 means filed; EFFECT is registration effectiveness, not an individual exchange-rule approval or trading. Generic listing standards are not a per-fund approval date.
3. Produce reviewed records matching `CryptoEtfRecord` in `src/lib/desk/types.ts`. Use null for unknown fields. Give each product a stable `id` and prior ids in `aliases`; an SEC accession identifies a document, not a product. Use CIK plus series id where supported; a registration file alone can cover several funds. Basket records have their constituent list and `productType: "basket"`; unknown weights stay null. Leveraged/inverse funds retain their exposure type. `multiAsset: true` represents an explicitly verified multi-asset mandate when constituents are not identified; it keeps that fund in the basket group without erasing leverage. Unknown constituent lists stay empty.
4. Every non-unverified status needs matching primary evidence: HTTPS `url`, `authority`, `title`, exact supporting `excerpt`, `asOf`, `verifiedAt`, and `status`. Record `statusAsOf` and `lastVerifiedAt` separately. The normalization guard downgrades unsupported statuses; it validates evidence metadata, **not the truth of a remotely supplied claim**. The source maintainer must review actual primary documents.
5. Import a reviewed catalog envelope (`schemaVersion: 1`, `scope: "US"`, `updatedAt`, `complete: false`, `coverageNote`, `records`) using `node --experimental-strip-types scripts/import-etf-catalog.mjs <file.json>`. This updates the bundled registry, preserves earlier products and merges dated lifecycle changes; inspect the diff and rebuild for publication. New asset groups require no React change.
6. For runtime updates, configure a trusted public normalized JSON feed with server-only `ETF_CATALOG_URL` and its exact hostname in comma-separated `ETF_CATALOG_HOSTS`. Include issuer proof hostnames in that list as well; SEC and supported official exchange hosts are built in. Allow those hostnames in environment networking. The ETF page's catalog refresh fetches through the backend, with time/size limits, no redirects, and the same validation. It independently fetches primary documents and matches the supporting excerpts before accepting new status evidence. Canonical SEC archive URLs use unpadded CIK path segments. Failed verification does not advance verification timestamps or replace earlier verified states. It preserves known records after errors or omission. An empty response never proves withdrawal. In-memory changes last only for the server process; use the reviewed import for durable published data. No worker or automatic freshness guarantee is implied.

7. Refresh official US listing evidence with `node --use-env-proxy --experimental-strip-types scripts/discover-etf-listings.mjs /tmp/listing-refresh.json`, then review the output and its separate candidate report before importing. Both NasdaqTrader directories must pass header, ETF/test flag, row and publication-date checks. Existing identified products receive dated listing evidence. New names remain unverified candidates with no guessed constituents, issuer or exposure. The reviewed corporate-equity exclusions are in `src/data/etf-listing-exclusions.json`. Discovery hints derive from all catalog assets, not a fixed coin list. This discovers name-based candidates; it cannot prove coverage of unnamed crypto strategies or newly filed funds. Continue SEC index and series review for filings.

The feed contract is a maintained registry of primary-evidence records, not an arbitrary issuer HTML scraper. The project does not yet have a verified full-market lifecycle provider. No new source record is shipped without verification; synthetic test fixtures are never loaded by the app.

## States and grouping

Approved/trading: approved, approved_not_launched, launched, trading. Active applications: filed, acknowledged, under_review, amended, delayed. Historical: withdrawn, rejected, delisting_filed. Form 25 exchange-removal notices are labeled `delisting_filed` (상장 폐지 신고); their filing alone does not assert an effective delisting date or completed liquidation. Unverified: inadequate/conflicting evidence or secondary flow observation.

Groups, asset/issuer selectors, searches, and counts derive from records. Baskets have one separate group and do not increase single-asset counts. Counts mean records verified as of their displayed source dates, not exhaustive current market totals. Two primary sources with conflicting statuses on the same date require review instead of silently choosing approval.

## Verified sources and remaining limits

The bundled registry was reviewed on 2026-10-10. Listing evidence uses the official NasdaqTrader `nasdaqlisted.txt` and `otherlisted.txt` snapshots published 2026-10-09 (ETF=Y, Test Issue=N). Product identities, exposure, constituents and lifecycle events use original SEC registration statements, prospectuses, periodic reports and withdrawal requests, plus official Grayscale, Canary, REX, Volatility Shares, ProShares and other issuer material. Each record links its actual documents and quoted evidence.

Filings were discovered through SEC 2025–2026 indexes and registrant/series histories. A filing or amendment is shown **as of its document date**, not asserted to be under active regulatory review today. Registration effectiveness and generic exchange listing rules do not establish individual product approval dates. OTC trust trading is kept distinct from an ETF uplisting application. Historical withdrawals are separate from active filing events.

The scope includes US crypto ETPs and direct crypto-linked futures, leveraged/inverse, income/options, mixed-asset and basket strategies. Corporate stock, mining, industry and adoption-themed funds without direct crypto exposure are excluded. Unknown launch/approval dates, issuer identities, exposure, constituents and weights remain visibly unknown. Listing evidence does not assert a live transaction or price.

Full US or global completeness is **not established**. Name-based directories may miss unnamed strategies, SEC indexes do not resolve every 1940 Act fund series, and an old filing can have an undiscovered subsequent event. The registry always reports `complete: false`. No runtime JSON provider is configured in this environment; the refresh button explains that limitation and preserves the reviewed registry. Import/discovery are executable update paths, not an automatic market-wide schedule. Source access remains subject to networking and issuer rate limits. In proxy-based environments, Node 24 `--use-env-proxy` (or `NODE_USE_ENV_PROXY=1`) uses the inherited proxy without disabling TLS verification.
